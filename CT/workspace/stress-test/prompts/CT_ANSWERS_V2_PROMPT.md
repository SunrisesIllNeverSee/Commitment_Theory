## What the Test Is Looking For (so you know what to draw from CT)

The test checks whether CT meets hard-science standards for a conservation law:

1. **Defined conserved quantity** — Is C(S) defined independently of the NLI oracle? Does it have units? Is it theory-independent? What's the minimal case?
2. **Symmetry/invariance** — What symmetry produces the conservation? Is it continuous? What's the Lagrangian equivalent? Does conservation fail when symmetry breaks?
3. **Independent measurement** — Is the NLI oracle independent of the LLM being measured? Can a different oracle get the same result? What's the uncertainty? Calibration standards?
4. **Falsifiability** — What specific observation kills the law? Was it pre-registered? Has anyone tried to falsify it? Law failure vs. instrument failure? Scope boundaries?
5. **Empirical asymmetry** — Governed vs. ungoverned conditions. Has the asymmetry been measured? Is it reproducible? Effect size? Novel predictions?

CT has material on most of these — the governed/ungoverned asymmetry is the core empirical claim, the oracle independence is addressed in Paper 5, the falsifiability is addressed in P-000 Proposition 11, the scope boundary is Proposition 11.3. The harder questions will be around units/dimension, the Noether symmetry, and the Lagrangian equivalent — CT may not have explicit answers to these, and that's important to surface honestly.
